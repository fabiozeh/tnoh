The Quiet Craft of Reading

On measure, leading, and the art of getting out of the way

By A. Typographer · July 9, 2026 · 6 min read

Good typography is invisible. You notice a great meal, a great song — but the best-set page simply disappears, leaving nothing between you and the words. This sampler exercises every element the stylesheet touches.

Reading is older than paper and far older than the screen, yet the screen has spent decades being hostile to it. We crammed text edge to edge, lit it with pure white light, and wondered why nobody finished the article. The fix is not clever — it is mostly restraint: a narrow column, generous leading, and colors that don't shout. This paragraph opens with a drop cap to show the flourish in context.

Consider the humble line length. Set text too wide and the eye loses its place on the return sweep; too narrow and it jerks along in fits. Around sixty-six characters is the comfortable middle, and it is why this column stops where it does. You can adjust it in one place — the --measure token.

Structure and rhythm #

Headings should feel like signposts, not billboards. Here the body is a serif and the headings a sans, a pairing that has anchored editorial design for a century. Below is a run of the elements you'll reach for most.

Code, inline and block

Inline snippets like const answer = 42; sit quietly in the text. Longer samples get a raised surface and scroll horizontally rather than wrapping and wrecking the rhythm: 

/* Toggle the theme and remember the choice */
function toggleTheme() {
  const root = document.documentElement;
  const dark = root.dataset.theme === 'dark';
  root.dataset.theme = dark ? 'light' : 'dark';
  localStorage.setItem('theme', root.dataset.theme);
}
